1 Core idea

We defend the system by simulating the attack first. We measure the real attack's yield against the unprotected data before building anything, and then we re-run that same attack against each candidate protected release before it ships — the attack is both our baseline and our test instrument. Testing your own locks before a burglar does.

2 The attack — findings/IDEA.md is the canonical account

A linkage attacker, not an intruder (IDEA.md:19): a curious analyst, broker or journalist holding several legitimately-obtained extracts from different controllers. They know every schema and published method; they do not know our keys.

Three join keys (IDEA.md:39-43): HETU (exact match), phone (normalise +35844… → 044… then match), IBAN (strip spaces, uppercase, match). The single precondition (IDEA.md:71): several releases still carry at least one of these or a reversible hash of one. Dropping patient_name does nothing.

Headline measured overlap: HETU health ∩ finance = 1622 people (IDEA.md:51; re-verified for this plan). Ground truth says 1623 people are genuinely co-present, so the HETU join recovers 99.9 % of them — a near-perfect key, not a heuristic. The other six measured joins (phone health∩finance 1484, phone health∩CS 2296, phone health∩energy 195, HETU health∩energy 194, IBAN energy∩finance 15, IBAN finance∩CS 150) are at IDEA.md:49-58.

Worked example, person P013174 (IDEA.md:60-64): ED patient Lehto Riku Viljami, diagnosis N20.0; the same HETU appears in finance as Riku Viljami Lehto with income_band 15-30k. Note the name order is reversed between the two files — name matching would have failed on this person, and the HETU still succeeds.

Why the pack's own scorer cannot see this: pets-eval scorecard takes one --original and one --protected, and run_scorecard fixes domain = original_dir.name (scorecard.py:290). Every entry in attack_specs.json and scenarios.json has a scalar "dataset". One domain per invocation, by signature — so no pack tool can express, let alone measure, a join between two of our own releases. The pack names the mosaic effect in prose (docs/THREAT_MODEL.md:9) but never operationalises it. That gap is what this MVP fills. Full derivation: MVP_Plan_original.md §1.

3 Requirements

Each requirement exists only because of a named attacker capability. The five load-bearing ones first.

IDFromRequirement
R3A3+A4 — exact-matches the keys across controllers (IDEA.md:75-76)No two outward releases may share a reversible identifier token for the same person. Satisfied by per-recipient keyed HMAC-SHA-256 with a distinct key domain per (dataset, scenario_id). This is the one requirement that closes the 1622 join.
R4The precondition's "or reversible hashes of them" (IDEA.md:71)No unsalted or unkeyed digest of HETU, phone, IBAN or e-mail may ever leave the controller. sha256(hetu) is enumerable over a tiny domain; the pack ships enumerate_hetu and hash_dictionary_attack to prove it. A keyed HMAC with a secret key is the only acceptable form.
R2A2 — attacker normalises before matching (IDEA.md:74)Our checker must normalise with the same recipe. Correctness test: it must reproduce 1622 and 1484 on raw data/. A checker that normalises differently under-counts and passes a vulnerable release.
R8A6 — confirms matches via adversary/public_directory.csv (IDEA.md:82)Every release must still pass pets-eval scorecard --scenario <ID>. Mosaic defence is additive to, never a substitute for, the pack's own checks.
R9A7 — success is the combination across releases (IDEA.md:83)A per-release green scorecard is not evidence of safety. We need a cross-release gate with a stated pass criterion and a positive control (§5, Part 1, component 6).

The remaining six, one line: read/write every key column as a string, never numeric-coerced (R1); where the scenario needs no stable person key, drop the identifier rather than tokenise it (R5); redact free text for the glue types, not only names (R6); apply each token domain consistently across every table (R7); keep keys outside the repo, gitignored, rotatable (R10); the key domain is (dataset, scenario_id), not dataset (R11). Full justifications: MVP_Plan_original.md §2.

4.3 Extending to a new scenario

Energy (ENERGY-S3) + customer_service (CS-S1) are the natural next pair — ENERGY-S3 carries the medical_device_priority payload, CS-S1 the widest glue (2296, the largest overlap). Re-pointing the system takes five steps, no new architecture:

  • Measure first — baseline join count on raw data before protecting anything.
  • Derive the requirement — add a row to §3: attack step → requirement → ID.
  • Extend, don't fork — add the pair to mosaic_check.py; record in project-owned files; resources/ never edited.
  • Re-point the same two gates — PASS-1 still demands 0, PASS-2 the new baseline.
  • Record the result, including failures — a check that found nothing means nothing unless its positive control passed.

Caveat: widening scope revives a risk absent from health+finance — glue surviving free-text redaction (turns.text: 5120 NATIONAL_ID, 4217 PHONE, 1473 IBAN gold spans; clinical_notes.note_text: 1637, 4877 — see §8).

Part 2 · safe to read with no technical background

Data flow, in plain language

Follow the arrows. No background needed.

[1] We take a copy of the hospital's visit records and the bank's customer list. We never
    work on the originals, and we check at the end that the originals are untouched.
 -> [2] We delete the things that point straight at one person: names, home addresses,
    e-mail addresses, telephone numbers.
 -> [3] The hospital's researchers still need to tell that two visits were the same
    patient, so we cannot simply delete the patient's ID number there. Instead we scramble
    it -- and we use a DIFFERENT scrambling recipe for the hospital's copy than for the
    bank's copy. The same person therefore comes out as two completely unrelated codes in
    the two files, so holding both files up side by side proves nothing. The bank's copy
    does not need a patient code at all, so there we delete the ID number outright.
 -> [4] We blur what is left: details that mean nothing alone but identify someone when
    combined. An exact birth date becomes an age range. An exact postcode becomes a wider
    area. A rare job title gets grouped with similar ones.
 -> [5] The two protected files go into a holding area. Nothing has been sent to anyone.
 -> [6] A robot now plays the burglar. It takes the two files out of the holding area and
    tries exactly what a real snooper would try: find the people who appear in both, and
    build one record showing a person's medical problem next to their income. This is
    testing your own locks before a burglar does.
 -> [7] The robot writes down whatever it managed to find, and then stops. It cannot change
    the files and it cannot approve anything.
 -> [8] A person reads the robot's report and decides. If the robot got through, we go back
    to step 2 with tighter settings and start again. If nobody reads the report, the answer
    is automatically no -- we never let an unread report count as approval.
 -> [9] Two fixed automatic tests run. They are the same every time and involve no robot
    and no judgement. The first counts how many people can be matched across the two
    protected files; the answer has to be none. The second runs that exact same count
    against the untouched originals, where it has to find the 1,622 people we already know
    are there -- because a broken counter would also say "none", and that is how we tell a
    real defence apart from a test that simply cannot see anything.
 -> [10] Only if both tests come out right do the two protected files leave the building.
    Handing them over is the one step that cannot be undone.

Workflow, one line each: a human supplies the config (dataset/scenario pairs, PET parameters, agent mode — keys stay in gitignored keys/); run_release.sh runs [1]-[5], the agent pass, then the gate, whose exit code is the script's; output lands in clean_data/<dataset>__<SID>/ plus the never-published rowmap_<table>.csv; make_benchmark.sh renders B1/B2 into benchmarks/.

7 Tech stack

Python, continuing the vendored scorer's own stack (pets-eval requires ≥3.10) — every net-new component reuses it directly: hmac/hashlib for the pseudonym service, pandas with dtype=str for R1, pets_eval.check's patterns and validators imported as a library, stdlib csv/re for the mosaic check.

matplotlib + seaborn for the two kept figures — B1, a paired bar of the join count before vs after (1622, 1484 → 0), and B2, privacy/utility/composite score per release, proving the fix didn't buy privacy by destroying utility. Both read every number from mosaic_check.json/scorecard.json; none typed by hand. Chosen over plotly because the output is static, non-interactive images — matplotlib's headless Agg backend needs no browser; plotly would need kaleido for the same result with nothing gained.

OPS-1: both scripts verify a committed SHA-256 manifest of the original data/ tree immediately before and after every run; any difference is a hard non-zero-exit failure, never a warning.

8 Residual risk

What the MVP does not close, stated plainly — a green mosaic check is a narrow claim.

IDResidual riskNext step
R-1Cross-release quasi-identifier mosaic. Keyed tokens break identifier joins but leave probabilistic matching on birth date / postcode / employer. The k targets are within-release only.Post-MVP: cross-release k-equivalence check
R-5Key compromise. A leaked keys/master.key restores linkability retroactively. Local gitignored keyfile, not a KMS, no rotation.Post-MVP: KMS + rotation schedule
R-6The pack's blind spot persists. A green mosaic_check.json means "these two releases don't join on the keys tested" — not "no cross-domain attack works".Ongoing: extend per §4.3's loop
R-9Minimisation is not audited. Nothing flags a column tokenised when it could have been dropped — and a tokenised key is join surface that outlives the key only as long as it stays secret.Post-MVP: a minimisation linter

Deliberately absent: R-2 (free-text leakage). Measured, not assumed: finance/customers.csv has no free-text column, and ed_visits's only one (chief_complaint) has zero glue-type gold spans in its 503 annotations. R-2 returns the moment energy/CS join scope (§4.3); R-9 took its place here because it's unmitigated and compounds R-5. R-2, R-3, R-4, R-7, R-8 are all in MVP_Plan_original.md §7.

The MVP's honest claim

Against an attacker with our two protected releases, no public adversary file, and no access to our keys, the measured cross-release join count on HETU, phone and IBAN is 0, down from a baseline of 1622 on the unprotected data — while each release still passes pets-eval scorecard for its own scenario.

Everything in the table above sits outside that sentence. That is the point of stating it so narrowly.
